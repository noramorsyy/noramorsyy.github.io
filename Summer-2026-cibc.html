Work Term Report Summer 2026

Business Systems Analyst Intern at CIBC

From May to August 2026, I continued my co-op at CIBC as a Business Systems Analyst Co-op on the Governance team within Infrastructure Operations & Networks, Technology Operations. Building on the experience I gained during my Winter 2026 term, I took on significantly more ownership of the Operational Support Information program. When the coworker who normally led much of the OSI work went on parental leave, I became responsible for managing the day-to-day OSI program and ensuring its regular governance activities continued smoothly.

Information About the Employer

CIBC is one of Canada’s major financial institutions, offering services across personal banking, business banking, wealth management and capital markets. CIBC also has a large technology organization that supports the systems, infrastructure, data and operations needed to keep banking services reliable and secure.

My role was connected to the technology side of the bank, specifically within Infrastructure Operations & Networks. The areas of computing most relevant to my work included data governance, infrastructure operations, data analysis, reporting, workflow support and stakeholder communication. I regularly used tools such as ServiceNow and Excel to manage OSI governance activities, analyze infrastructure-related data and organize information for reporting and decision-making.

Goals

Goal 1 - Technological Literacy

My first goal was to move beyond simply using ServiceNow and Excel for assigned tasks and become more independent in using these tools to manage an ongoing governance process. During the term, I used ServiceNow to export and review OSI data, create lists for audit and stakeholder requests, and organize information needed for OSI reviews. I also maintained the OSI Tracker in Excel, updated formulas and tracking logic, monitored overdue items and prepared monthly reporting. Taking ownership of the OSI program required me to understand how the different tools, data sources and recurring tasks connected to one another rather than treating them as separate activities. By the end of the term, I was able to manage these tools with much less supervision and use them to support the full OSI workflow.

Goal 2 - Critical & Creative Thinking: Inquiry and Analysis

My second goal was to strengthen my ability to identify issues in governance data and determine what action was required. Managing the OSI program meant continuously reviewing records for missing information, overdue completions, inconsistencies and items that required escalation. Instead of only identifying a problem, I had to determine who needed to be contacted, what information was missing and how the issue should be tracked through to completion. I also continued supporting EOL/EOS analysis for servers and applications, using pivot tables, Excel formulas and ServiceNow exports to identify gaps, affected environments and mitigation strategies. This helped me improve my ability to connect data findings to governance, security, compliance and operational risk.

Goal 3 - Communicating: Written and Verbal Communication

My third goal was to become more confident communicating independently with stakeholders. Because I was managing the OSI program while my coworker was on parental leave, I handled regular OSI review emails, escalation messages, shared mailbox questions, follow-ups and information requests. I also prepared monthly slide decks and communicated OSI status and outstanding items during governance discussions. This required me to be clear, accurate and professional because the information was being used by different teams and senior stakeholders. Over the term, I became more comfortable deciding how much detail was needed, explaining issues clearly and following up without relying on someone else to manage the communication.

Goal 4 - Professional & Ethical Behaviour: Leadership and Responsibility

My fourth goal was to develop stronger ownership, accountability and leadership skills. The biggest change from my Winter 2026 term was that I was no longer only supporting the OSI process. When my coworker went on parental leave, I took over the day-to-day operation of the OSI program. I was responsible for keeping the tracker current, sending reviews and escalations, monitoring the shared mailbox, responding to questions, preparing reporting and making sure recurring OSI activities were completed on time. I still worked closely with my team and asked for guidance when appropriate, but I had much more responsibility for organizing my own work, prioritizing issues and keeping the process moving. This experience helped me become more confident taking ownership of an important governance process and being accountable for its continuity.

Job Description and Highlights

Position: Business Systems Analyst Co-op

Team: Governance — Infrastructure Operations & Networks, Technology Operations

Term: Summer 2026 Semester

During my Summer 2026 co-op term, I continued working as a Business Systems Analyst Co-op on the Governance team within Infrastructure Operations & Networks, Technology Operations. This term was a continuation of my Winter 2026 placement, but my responsibilities increased significantly. The largest change was taking over the day-to-day management of the Operational Support Information (OSI) program while my coworker was on parental leave. This gave me the opportunity to move from supporting the process to independently coordinating many of its recurring governance activities.

Taking Ownership of the OSI Program

During my previous term, I learned the OSI process by helping maintain the tracker, sending reviews and escalations, monitoring the shared mailbox and supporting monthly reporting. In Summer 2026, I became responsible for managing these activities on an ongoing basis. I maintained the OSI Tracker, monitored upcoming and overdue items, sent review requests and escalation messages, followed up with stakeholders, responded to questions through the shared mailbox and ensured outstanding items continued moving toward completion.

Taking ownership of the program required more than completing individual tasks. I had to understand the overall workflow, keep track of multiple items at different stages, prioritize time-sensitive issues and make sure important follow-ups were not missed. I also had to recognize when I could resolve an issue independently and when it needed to be brought to another member of the governance team. This was one of the most valuable parts of the term because it gave me experience managing a real operational process with less day-to-day supervision.

OSI Reporting, Reviews and Escalations

I continued using ServiceNow and Excel extensively throughout the OSI process. I exported OSI data from ServiceNow, organized it in Excel, maintained the OSI Tracker and used formulas to help identify overdue completions and items requiring escalation. I also prepared monthly slide decks and summarized OSI status for governance discussions.

I handled regular stakeholder communication related to OSI reviews, missing information, overdue items and escalations. Because I was managing the program more independently, I became more comfortable reviewing the available information first, determining what was required and then sending a clear request or follow-up. This helped make the process more organized and improved my confidence in communicating with stakeholders across the bank.

Audit and Information Requests

Another part of the OSI program involved responding to audit-related and stakeholder information requests. I used ServiceNow to create lists and export the requested information, then organized the data in Excel so it could be reviewed and shared in a useful format. These requests reinforced the importance of keeping OSI records accurate and complete because the same governance data may later be used for reporting, audits or risk-related reviews.

EOL/EOS Governance Analysis

I also continued supporting end-of-life and end-of-support governance analysis for servers and applications. I used pivot tables, Excel formulas and ServiceNow exports to review EOL/EOS data related to Server Hardening Requirements and Patch for Security and Enhancements. The analysis helped identify gaps, affected environments and mitigation strategies.

Continuing this work alongside my OSI responsibilities helped me strengthen my ability to manage both recurring operational work and analysis-based assignments. It also gave me a better understanding of how infrastructure data connects to governance, security, compliance and operational risk.

Collaboration and Stakeholder Communication

Although I had greater ownership this term, the work still required close collaboration with my team and with stakeholders across CIBC. I reached out to different teams about OSIs, answered questions, followed up on outstanding actions and escalated items when required. I also worked with members of my governance team when an issue required additional context or a decision beyond my responsibility.

This balance between independence and collaboration was an important part of my development. I learned that taking ownership does not mean working alone; it means being accountable for moving work forward while knowing when to involve the right people.

Professional Development

Returning to the same team for another term also gave me the opportunity to build stronger working relationships and develop a deeper understanding of CIBC’s technology organization. Since I already understood the basic processes and tools, I was able to spend more time learning why the governance processes existed, how different teams used the information and how my work affected broader operational and risk-management activities.

Skills Used and Developed

This term helped me strengthen both technical and professional skills, including ServiceNow, Excel, pivot tables, Excel formulas, XLOOKUPs, data analysis, data visualization, governance tracking, reporting, presentations, stakeholder communication, prioritization, independent problem-solving, program ownership, teamwork and professional documentation. Compared with my previous term, the biggest area of growth was learning how to take responsibility for an ongoing governance program and manage its recurring activities from beginning to end.

Conclusions

My Summer 2026 term was an important step forward from my Winter 2026 co-op because I was able to build on the technical knowledge I had already developed and take on much greater responsibility. The most significant experience was taking over the day-to-day OSI program while my coworker was on parental leave. This required me to manage recurring reviews, escalations, tracking, stakeholder communication, reporting and information requests with greater independence.

Through this experience, I learned that successful governance work depends not only on accurate data and technical tools, but also on organization, consistency, communication and accountability.
